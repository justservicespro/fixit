Personal Protective Equipment: A Practical Guide for Technicians and Workshops

By FixIt Abuja·18 September 2026·3 min read

Whether you're an electrician, a generator technician, or someone doing occasional DIY repairs at home, the right protective equipment is the difference between a close call and an actual injury.

PPE isn't about looking cautious — it's about making sure the one time something goes wrong, it doesn't go badly wrong.

Matching PPE to the task

Electrical work

  • Insulated gloves rated for the voltage involved.
  • Safety glasses to protect against arc flash or debris.
  • Non-conductive footwear, especially on damp or concrete floors.

Generator and mechanical work

  • Ear protection around running generators — hearing damage from prolonged exposure is cumulative and permanent.
  • Gloves suited to handling hot components and fuel.
  • Steel-toe boots when moving heavy units.

General workshop and repair work

  • Safety glasses whenever cutting, grinding or drilling.
  • A dust mask when working with insulation, dust-heavy environments or fibreglass.
  • A hard hat on any site with overhead work or falling-object risk.
⚠ Safety

PPE reduces risk — it doesn't eliminate it. Continue following safe procedures (isolating power, using the right tools, working with a second person on higher-risk jobs) even while wearing the correct protective equipment.

Why it gets skipped, and why that's costly

PPE gets skipped most often because it's inconvenient in the moment — gloves slow down fine work, glasses fog up, ear protection is easy to forget. But the cost of an eye injury, a burn, or long-term hearing damage is measured in far more than the minor inconvenience of putting the gear on.

✓ Do

  • Keep PPE stored somewhere it's actually easy to grab before starting a job.
  • Replace damaged or degraded gloves and glasses rather than continuing to use them.
  • Train anyone working under you on which PPE applies to which task.
  • Check that PPE genuinely fits — oversized gloves or loose glasses don't protect properly.

✕ Don't

  • Don't treat PPE as optional for 'quick' jobs — most injuries happen on jobs assumed to be quick.
  • Don't reuse damaged protective gear because a replacement isn't immediately on hand.
  • Don't skip ear protection around generators, assuming short exposure is harmless — it accumulates.
  • Don't let convenience override protection on electrical work specifically; the margin for error there is genuinely small.

Key takeaways

  • Match PPE to the specific task, not a one-size-fits-all kit.
  • Damaged protective gear should be replaced, not stretched further.
  • Hearing and eye damage from skipped PPE is often permanent and cumulative.
  • The inconvenience of PPE is always smaller than the cost of the injury it prevents.

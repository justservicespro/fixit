How to Clear a Printer Paper Jam Without Making It Worse

By FixIt Abuja·19 August 2026·3 min read
Share:

A paper jam is rarely a sign your printer is dying — it's usually a sign of dust, worn rollers, or paper that's slightly damp, curled or the wrong weight.

The way you remove the jammed sheet matters as much as removing it at all. Pulling roughly is how a simple jam turns into a torn roller or a cracked internal gear.

The safe way to clear a jam

  1. Switch the printer off and unplug it before reaching inside.
  2. Open every access panel the manufacturer provides — front, rear and top — rather than forcing paper out through one opening.
  3. Gently pull the jammed sheet in the direction of normal paper travel, not backwards against the rollers.
  4. Check for small torn pieces left behind; these cause the very next jam if missed.
  5. Close all panels fully before powering back on — many printers won't run with a panel ajar.

  • Paper stored somewhere humid, causing sheets to stick together.
  • Overfilling the paper tray beyond its marked limit.
  • Mixing paper weights or sizes in the same tray.
  • Dust and paper fibre build-up on the feed rollers over time.
  • A worn separation pad that's no longer gripping single sheets properly.

Tip: Fan a new stack of paper before loading it — a quick riffle through the edges separates sheets that have stuck together in storage, which is one of the single biggest causes of jams.

⚠ Safety

Never reach into a printer with the power still connected, and never use a metal tool to dig out a jam — both can damage internal electronics or injure your fingers on moving rollers.

When it's a parts problem, not a paper problem

If jams are happening several times a week despite good paper and a clean tray, the feed rollers or separation pad are likely worn and need replacing — a straightforward job for a technician, and far cheaper than living with constant jams.

Key takeaways

  • Always power off before reaching inside a printer.
  • Pull jammed paper in the direction it normally travels, never backwards.
  • Humid storage and overfilled trays cause more jams than the printer itself.
  • Frequent jams despite good paper usually mean worn rollers, not bad luck.

Buying a Generator in Nigeria: Do's and Don'ts Before You Spend a Kobo

By FixIt Abuja·04 August 2026·3 min read

A generator is one of the bigger purchases most Nigerian households and small businesses make, and getting it wrong is expensive twice over — once at purchase, and again in fuel and repairs for years afterward.

Before you hand over any money, here's what actually separates a good buy from a costly mistake.

✓ Do

  • Calculate your actual load in watts before shopping, including the startup surge of fridges and AC units.
  • Buy a little above your calculated need — running a generator at 50–80% load is more efficient and lasts longer.
  • Ask specifically about after-sales support and where spare parts are sold locally.
  • Check the fuel tank capacity against how long you actually need it to run unattended.
  • Test-start the unit in the shop before you pay, if at all possible.

✕ Don't

  • Don't buy purely on price — the cheapest unit is often the most expensive over 2–3 years in repairs.
  • Don't undersize it to save money; overloading a generator shortens its life dramatically.
  • Don't buy without checking if a local technician stocks parts for that brand.
  • Don't ignore noise level if you live close to neighbours — soundproof casing costs more but avoids real friction.
  • Don't skip asking for a warranty in writing, however informal the seller seems.

Petrol, diesel, or inverter — which one fits?

Petrol generators

Best for smaller, occasional loads — a few rooms, a router, some lights and a fridge. Cheaper upfront, noisier, and petrol is generally pricier per hour of runtime than diesel.

Inverter generators

Quieter and more fuel-efficient for smaller loads, with cleaner power output that's safer for sensitive electronics. A strong option if noise or laptop/TV safety is a priority.

Sizing it correctly

Add up the running wattage of everything you'll power at once, then add the highest single startup surge (usually a fridge or AC compressor) on top. A generator sized to your everyday load, with headroom for the occasional heavier day, beats one bought purely on budget.

⚠ Safety

Never buy a generator without checking it has a proper earthing point and that your installation includes a change-over switch — running a generator directly into your house wiring without one can feed power back into the grid and is dangerous to utility workers as well as your own household.

Key takeaways

  • Size the generator to your real load plus the biggest startup surge.
  • Match fuel type to how often and how long you'll actually run it.
  • Parts availability matters as much as brand name.
  • A proper change-over switch and earthing are non-negotiable, not optional extras.

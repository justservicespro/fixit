AC Dos and Don'ts: Get More Cooling for Less Power

By FixIt Abuja·10 August 2026·3 min read
Share:

Small daily habits around how an AC is used affect both your power bill and how often it needs repair — often more than the brand or model does.

✓ Do

  • Set the thermostat to 24–26°C rather than the coldest setting; it cools nearly as fast and uses noticeably less power.
  • Close doors and windows in the room while the AC runs.
  • Give the outdoor unit shade if possible — direct afternoon sun makes it work harder.
  • Service it before the hottest months, not after cooling has already dropped off.
  • Use a stabilizer or the generator's clean-power mode if you're running the AC off backup power.

✕ Don't

  • Don't set it to the lowest possible temperature thinking it will cool faster — the compressor just runs harder for longer.
  • Don't block the indoor or outdoor unit with furniture, curtains or stored items.
  • Don't switch the AC on and off repeatedly within a few minutes — give the compressor at least 3–5 minutes to rest.
  • Don't run an old, poorly maintained unit on an undersized generator; the startup surge can damage both.
  • Don't ignore a rising power bill with no other explanation — it's often an early sign the AC is struggling.

Why the thermostat setting matters so much

Every degree colder you set the thermostat below about 24°C makes the compressor work disproportionately harder, since it's fighting a bigger gap between room and outdoor temperature. Most rooms feel comfortably cool at 24–26°C with a ceiling fan running alongside.

Protecting the AC on generator or inverter power

AC compressors draw a sharp surge of power at startup — often 2–3 times their running wattage. If you're on generator or inverter backup, make sure the system is sized for that surge, and stagger the AC's startup rather than switching on several units at once.

Tip: If you're leaving a room for more than 30–60 minutes, it's usually more efficient to switch the AC off rather than leave it running low — the exception is if the outside temperature is extreme and the room needs to stay pre-cooled for a returning household.

Key takeaways

  • 24–26°C cools nearly as well as the coldest setting, for less strain and power.
  • Give the compressor a few minutes rest between on/off cycles.
  • Shade and airflow around the outdoor unit protect efficiency.
  • Size your backup power for the AC's startup surge, not just its running load.

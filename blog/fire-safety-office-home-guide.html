Fire Safety Basics Every Nigerian Home and Office Should Practice

By FixIt Abuja·03 September 2026·3 min read
Share:

Most house and office fires in Nigeria start from causes that are entirely preventable: overloaded sockets, unattended cooking, faulty wiring, and generators placed too close to structures.

Fire safety isn't about fear — it's about a handful of habits and a plan you rarely need, but are glad to have the one time you do.

Prevention comes first

  • Never leave cooking unattended, especially oil-based frying.
  • Keep generators at least a few metres from windows, doors and flammable material, with clear ventilation.
  • Don't overload sockets or leave damaged cables in use.
  • Store flammable liquids like petrol and kerosene away from heat sources and out of living areas.
  • Test smoke alarms if fitted, and consider adding them if not.

✓ Do

  • Water extinguishers — general household fires (wood, paper, fabric), never electrical or oil fires.
  • CO2 extinguishers — safe for electrical fires, doesn't leave residue.
  • Dry powder extinguishers — versatile, good general-purpose choice for home or office.
  • Foam extinguishers — effective on both general and liquid fires.

✕ Don't

  • Never use water on an electrical fire or a cooking oil fire — both can make it dramatically worse.
  • Never assume a fire extinguisher is ready without checking its pressure gauge periodically.
  • Never store an extinguisher somewhere it becomes inaccessible during an actual emergency.
  • Never fight a fire that's already spreading beyond its starting point — get out and call for help instead.
⚠ Safety

If a fire is spreading, is blocking your exit, or you're not confident you can control it in the first few seconds with the right extinguisher, leave immediately, close doors behind you to slow the spread, and call for help from a safe distance. No possession is worth the risk of staying.

A simple home or office escape plan

  1. Identify two ways out of every room, where possible.
  2. Agree a single outdoor meeting point everyone in the household or office knows.
  3. Keep exit routes clear of stored furniture, generators or clutter.
  4. Walk through the plan with children or staff so it's familiar, not just written down.

Tip: Check the pressure gauge on your fire extinguisher every few months — a discharged or expired extinguisher gives false confidence exactly when you can least afford it.

Key takeaways

  • Most fires start from preventable causes — cooking, sockets, generator placement.
  • Match the extinguisher type to the fire; water on an electrical or oil fire makes things worse.
  • Know two exits and one meeting point, and keep them clear.
  • If in doubt, get out — property can be replaced.

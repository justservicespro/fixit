Why Your Laptop Overheats — And What Actually Fixes It

By FixIt Abuja·12 September 2026·3 min read

A laptop that's hot to the touch, loud, or randomly shutting down under load is almost always a cooling problem, not a sign the machine itself is failing.

The good news is that overheating is one of the most fixable laptop problems, and the fix is usually cheaper than people expect.

The usual suspects, in order of likelihood

1. Dust-clogged fans and vents

Dust builds up on the internal fan and heat sink fins over months of use, restricting airflow exactly where the laptop needs it most. This alone accounts for a large share of overheating complaints.

2. Blocked intake vents

Using a laptop on a soft surface like a bed or a cushion blocks the underside intake vents, starving the cooling system of airflow even if the internals are clean.

3. Dried-out thermal paste

The thermal paste between the processor and heat sink degrades over a few years, reducing heat transfer even with clean fans. This needs a technician to safely reapply.

4. Background software load

Too many background programs or browser tabs push the processor harder than necessary, generating more heat even with a healthy cooling system.

What you can check yourself

  • Use the laptop on a hard, flat surface, never a soft one that blocks vents.
  • Listen for the fan running constantly and loudly even during light tasks — a sign of restricted airflow.
  • Check for dust visibly built up at the vent openings.
  • Close unnecessary background programs and browser tabs.
⚠ Safety

Never open a laptop's casing to clean internal components yourself unless you're confident with the specific model — modern laptops have delicate ribbon cables and battery connections that are easy to damage without the right guide.

Tip: A laptop cooling pad with its own fan can meaningfully reduce heat build-up as a stopgap, but it treats the symptom — an internal clean is still worth doing if the fan is constantly loud.

When to take it to a technician

  • The laptop shuts down unexpectedly under normal use.
  • The fan runs loudly even when the machine is idle.
  • It's more than 2–3 years old and has never had an internal clean.
  • You can feel intense heat concentrated in one spot on the casing.

Key takeaways

  • Dust build-up is the single most common cause of laptop overheating.
  • Always use a laptop on a hard surface with clear vents.
  • Thermal paste needs professional replacement every few years.
  • Sudden shutdowns under load are a clear signal to get it serviced, not ignored.

Getting Your Generator Ready for Harmattan and the Dry Season

By FixIt Abuja·27 September 2026·3 min read

Dust is the dry season's version of what rain is to the wet season for a generator — a slow, steady cause of clogged filters, overheating and reduced performance if left unmanaged.

Harmattan's fine dust gets into air filters and radiator fins faster than most people expect, and combined with higher ambient temperatures, it puts real extra stress on the cooling system.

Before harmattan sets in

  1. Clean or replace the air filter, even if it doesn't look overdue yet.
  2. Clear dust from radiator fins and cooling vents thoroughly.
  3. Check coolant levels on water-cooled units before the heat picks up.
  4. Cover the generator when not in use, without blocking ventilation while it's actually running.
  5. Inspect the battery — heat and dust both shorten battery life faster than mild weather.

  • Check and clean the air filter more frequently than your usual schedule — weekly in heavy dust conditions.
  • Watch for reduced power output, a common early sign of a dust-clogged filter.
  • Keep the generator's running area as dust-free as practically possible, away from loose sand or ongoing construction dust.
⚠ Safety

A generator covered while running, or covered with something that blocks its ventilation to keep dust out, is a serious overheating and carbon monoxide risk. Any dust cover must only be used while the unit is off.

Heat stress on top of dust

Higher dry-season temperatures mean the generator's cooling system has less margin for error. A dust-clogged radiator combined with high ambient heat is one of the more common causes of dry-season generator failures — both problems compound each other.

Tip: If your generator runs in a dusty compound near a road or building site, consider a simple, well-ventilated dust screen around (not over) the unit — it meaningfully cuts filter clogging between services without restricting airflow.

Key takeaways

  • Harmattan dust clogs filters and radiators faster than most service schedules assume.
  • Increase filter checks to weekly during heavy dust periods.
  • Never cover a generator while it's running, even to keep dust off.
  • Dust and heat stress compound each other — both need managing together in the dry season.
